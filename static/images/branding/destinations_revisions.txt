## DESTINATION 4 — GUMATONG SHOAL

**Location:** Panukulan, Quezon

**Tagline:**  
"The Shoal That Speaks Before the Storm"

**About This Destination:**

Gumatong Shoal is a remarkable marine formation associated with the waters of Panukulan, Quezon. More than a scenic coastal attraction, it holds a special place in local fishing knowledge. The shoal helps break powerful waves, and local fishers have traditionally listened to the sounds created when waves strike its rocks as a sign of approaching storms. During suitable low-tide conditions, parts of its rocks and sand become visible in the surrounding waters. Gumatong reflects the connection between coastal communities, the sea, and traditional environmental knowledge.

**What to Explore:**
- Natural shoal formations
- Rocks and sand visible at suitable tides
- Coastal and marine scenery
- Landscape photography
- Traditional fishing and weather knowledge

**Before You Go:**

Coordinate with local guides and respect marine conservation rules. Do not promise snorkeling, diving, or unrestricted access.

## DESTINATION 5 — PULONG KUKOK

**Location:** Polillo Group of Islands, Quezon

**Known Alternative Names:** Cocooc Shoal; Snake Island

**Tagline:**  
"Where the Rocks Resemble a Rooster"

**About This Destination:**

Pulong Kukok is one of the Polillo island group's unusual natural attractions. Known for its white sandbar, clear waters, and two large rock formations resembling a rooster, the island has a character unlike an ordinary beach. Its name is connected to the sound of a rooster, while its alternative name, Snake Island, reflects the presence of sea snakes around its rocky habitat. Pulong Kukok offers visitors a memorable combination of island scenery, natural rock formations, and wildlife significance.

**What to Explore:**
- Rooster-shaped rock formations
- White sandbar
- Clear coastal waters
- Island photography
- Wildlife observation from a safe distance

**Before You Go:**

Sea snakes may be present. Do not approach or disturb wildlife. Visits should be coordinated with local operators and timed around safe sea and tidal conditions.
